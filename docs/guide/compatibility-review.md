# Natural-language compatibility review

This review covers the supplied English/French examples and all eight [edtfy test files](https://github.com/nicompte/edtfy/tree/master/tests), including its disabled shared-component collection examples. Selected edtfy examples are credited to Nicolas Barbotte (MIT). We use the examples to identify syntax gaps; their older EDTF outputs are not a conformance oracle.

The tables are generated by `node packages/natural/scripts/generate-compatibility.cjs`. The phrase expectations live in `packages/natural/tests/fixtures/compatibility.json` and are enforced by `compatibility.test.ts`. English numeric examples use `en-US`, French uses `fr-FR`, and rolling short years use January 1, 2026. All alternatives are checked, in confidence order. Separate shared fixtures and round-trip tests cover Spanish and all six regional locale presets.

## Corrected patterns

- A known day survives an unspecified month: `1870-XX-12` renders as **12th of unknown month, 1870**, **día 12 de mes desconocido, 1870**, or **12 d'un mois inconnu, 1870**. These phrases parse back without losing the day.
- Dates and seasons share interval endpoints: **march 1988 - spring 1990** becomes `1988-03/1990-21`. Reversed intervals are rejected. Season calendar bounds remain application conventions.
- French **De … à …**, articles, and apostrophe contractions work with shared date semantics. Season qualifiers work before or after the season. Century-wide qualifiers apply to both boundaries.
- Missing-year forms, semicolon lists, short range end years, ordinal/roman centuries, selected abbreviations, and validated weekday prefixes have regression coverage. **January 12** ranks `XXXX-01-12` first, retaining `0012-01` as an ambiguous alternative; **January 0012** selects the historical month/year.

## Modern EDTF distinctions

The [current LOC specification](https://www.loc.gov/standards/datetime/) replaced `u` with `X`, combined `?~` into `%`, and replaced interval keywords with empty/`..` endpoints. We do not accept obsolete literal spellings as modern EDTF.

**Since 1970** means `1970/..`; an unknown end is `1970/` and renders explicitly as unknown. **20th century** means `1901/2000`, whereas `19XX` covers 1900–1999. **January 1999 to December 1999** is an interval, not a synonym for the unspecified month `1999-XX`. **2020; 2021** is an inclusive list, `{2020..2021}`; bare `2020,2021` is not valid EDTF. Strict event-date cutoffs use sets and exclude the cutoff unit. Fuzzy cutoff phrases remain errors.

Bare short years preserve historical values: **90** means `0090`. Write **1990** explicitly or use **'90** for the rolling window. Short end years in a range inherit the start century, advancing to the next century if necessary: **1851-52** → `1851/1852`. Valid literal EDTF takes precedence: `1851-12` remains December 1851.

## Remaining scope differences

The upstream tests include incorrect weekdays and reversed intervals; we reject them. Weekday prefixes constrain the full calendar date instead of being discarded. Draft `u`/`*` slash masks should be converted explicitly to modern EDTF with `X`; extended years remain year-only in the selected profile.

Shared-component collection syntax such as **spring or summer 2015** and **April 1 or 2 2015** remains outside this change (these cases are disabled in edtfy's suites). Use explicit members, such as **One of: spring 2015, summer 2015** or **One of: April 1, 2015, April 2, 2015**. A future extension needs contextual component propagation through all collection members and language-specific word order, rather than guessing a year from the last number.

Bibliographic event labels such as **printed** are not discarded. Parse each event separately so dates and event roles are retained. A day within an approximate season cannot be encoded by appending `-XX` to a season code; use season precision if sufficient, or explicit calendar dates. No broad text-stripping or obsolete-EDTF compatibility mode was added.
